Plagiarism Checker (Compare Texts Free)

Check an essay, article or assignment for copied passages. Paste your text and the sources you want to compare it with (articles, web pages, other essays); the checker highlights every passage that appears in a source and shows the percentage. It also builds exact-phrase web searches for your most distinctive sentences.

How to check text for plagiarism

  1. Paste the text you want to check, or load a .txt or .docx file.
  2. Paste one or more sources to compare against, or load them from files.
  3. Choose how many consecutive matching words count as a match (8 is a good default).
  4. Press Check. Matching passages are highlighted per source, with a similarity percentage.
  5. Use the Google, Bing or DuckDuckGo links to search the web for your most distinctive sentences.

How the check works

Both texts are split into overlapping word sequences, ignoring capitals and punctuation, and each sequence is turned into a fingerprint. Wherever at least the chosen number of consecutive words in your text also appears in a source, that passage is highlighted. This is the same idea used by academic tools such as MOSS, applied to the sources you provide.

What it doesn't do

It does not scan the whole internet or private databases of student papers, as paid services such as Turnitin do. Instead, it gives you one-click exact-phrase searches for the sentences most likely to reveal copying. It also finds copied wording, not ideas that were reworded, so always read the highlighted passages yourself.

Private

Your text and sources are compared in your browser and are never uploaded or stored, so unpublished work stays yours.

Questions

Does this plagiarism checker search the internet?

Not automatically. It compares your text with the sources you add and gives you exact-phrase search links for your most distinctive sentences, which open in Google, Bing or DuckDuckGo.

What counts as a match?

A run of at least the chosen number of consecutive words (8 by default) that appears in a source, ignoring capitals and punctuation. Shorter common phrases are ignored to avoid false alarms.

Is it free and private?

Yes. There is no sign-up, and nothing you paste leaves your browser.

Can it detect paraphrased plagiarism?

Only partly. Heavy rewording breaks up exact word sequences. Use the highlights and web searches as a guide, and read the sources yourself.

Can I check a Word document?

Yes. Load a .docx or .txt file for your text or for any source.
